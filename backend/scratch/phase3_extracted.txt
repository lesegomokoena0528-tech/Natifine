3. Analysis Phase 
 
3.1 Introduction 
The Planning Phase (Phase 2) established that traffic fine administration in South 
Africa is fragmented, address -based and largely manual, and it confirmed that a 
centralised, ID-linked prototype called NatiFine SA is  necessary, technically 
achievable, operationally beneficial and economically viable. Phase 3 builds on those 
findings. Whereas Phase 2 answered the question “should we build this and how will 
we organise the work?”. The Analysis Phase answers, “what exactly must the system 
do, and what must it store?”. 
The purpose of this phase is to study the current way in which fines are issued, 
communicated, paid and disputed and to identify the data the proposed system must 
hold and the rules that keep that data correct, to expose the weaknesses of the current 
arrangement, and to translate these findings into clear functional and non -functional 
requirements and a data model. The output of this phase is the input to the Design 
phase,  so every requirement is written to be testable. 
This chapter is organized as follows: 
• 3.2 Information gathering methodology: how facts about the current system 
were collected. 
• 3.3 Analysis of the existing system: how fines are handled today. 
• 3.4 Data analysis: the data to be stored, its integrity rules and constraints. 
• 3.5 Weaknesses of the current system. 
• 3.6 Analysis of the proposed system: functional requirements. 
• 3.7 Non-Functional requirements. 
• 3.8 Data modelling for the proposed system. 
Scope reminder. As agreed in Phase 2, NatiFine SA is an academic prototype built 
with Python, HTML/CSS, JavaScript and SQLite. Payments, SMS and WhatsApp 
delivery and government data are simulated whereas  all fines, motorists and payment 
details are used for demonstration and are fictitious. 
 
3.2 Information Gathering Methodology 
The methodology for the project NatiFine SA is based on preliminary research, 
analysis of available information in public domain, as well as evaluation of needs 
identified in Phases 1 and 2. There were no actual interviews with motorists, traffic 
officers or municipal officials carried out. No interview outcomes were presented or 
indicated that feedback was gained from external representatives in the analysis. 
Instead, project documents and information available in public domain were utilized 
for problem understanding and project requirements identification. 
--- PAGE 3 ---
 
The methodology proved to be quite relevant for the academic purposes of this 
project, as the educators had to understand the concept of traffic fines, point out 
major weaknesses and figure out basic functions of the prototype. 
 
3.2.1 Observation 
 
The team assessed the problem statement, project proposal and planning documentation 
which were prepared in the First and Second phases of the project. This documentation 
stated that fragmented activities are accompanied by problems, and a solution was 
proposed in the form of the required digital platform where drivers will be able to see their 
fines, perform payment simulations, file complaints and get notifications. 
Publicly available information on the traffic violations in South Africa was used in order to get 
an idea of the basic stages of dealing with the infringement notifications. The guidance on 
the Administrative Adjudication of Road Traffic Offences published by the South African 
Government states such processes as making payments and declaring the incident 
(Government, 2010). Such information was useful while analyzing the processes that will be 
demonstrated in the prototype. 
The disadvantage of the given method is that public materials do not give insight into local 
internal procedures, nor do they guarantee that all government bodies have the same 
procedures. As a result, the derived knowledge is going to be used in the process of 
establishing the requirements of the prototype but not as an argument for claiming that every 
traffic authority operates identically. 
3.2.2 Participatory Method 
The project group made an analysis of issues and targets as described in Phase 1 
documentation and Phase 2 design papers. These issues included the problem of 
places where fines could be displayed, a need for the clear payment and dispute 
system, the necessity for reminders and accessible information about fines. 
Then all the requirements were transformed into system functions, such as the 
registration of motorists and login, fine display, demonstration of payment 
operations, submission of disputes, sending notifications and reminders, and 
interaction with an admin. The limitations of the project were kept in mind as well – 
the use of fictional data and lack of real government databases, banking and 
SMS/WhatsApp gates. 
This way, the team made sure that their analysis was consistent with the official 
project outline and the Agile methodology in question. However, at this stage, as 
formal interviews were not conducted, and external user testing was not performed 
yet, the project group cannot state that the requirements were validated with the help 
of representative groups.

3.2.3 Interviews 
The primary disadvantage was the lack of formal interviews and direct confirmations 
from the municipal traffic authorities. As a result, the team was not able to verify 
independently all user experiences or internal administrative procedures. The results 
are based on the materials gained from the project and available public information 
and hence should be taken in this context.  
It would be possible to acquire further feedback during a later stage of the Agile 
development process by approaching the supervisor and, if feasible, prospective 
users regarding the usability of the prototypes. Any feedback obtained will be 
recorded accordingly and used to adjust the requirements.
 
Summary of Findings 
The preliminary research has shown that there is a need in the market for a solution 
to integrate key functions of fine management to one interface. The proposed 
technology will allow drivers to see the fines associated with their accounts, simulate 
payment processes, make claims and receive notifications. An officer or an 
administrator will be tasked with entering fines and managing disputes. 
The findings are based on initial project research and document review and are not 
supported by completed interviews and user studies. They form the basis for the 
existing process analysis; weaknesses identified in section 3.5 and the requirements 
for the new system proposed in section 3.6.

3.3 Analysis of the Existing System. 
The existing arrangement is not one system but several loosely related ones in 
which individual municipalities and metropolitan police departments each run their 
own fine-management systems which are supported by the national AARTO 
framework and the eNatis licensing environment. These do not share a common, ID-
based record of a motorist’s fines.

3.4 Data Analysis (Data Integrity and Constraints) 
Data analysis identifies the information that the NatiFine SA prototype must store, the 
sources of that information and the rules required to maintain its accuracy, consistency 
and security. The system stores motorist details, traffic -fine records, payment  
information, dispute submissions and notification histories. Appropriate integrity rules 
are necessary to prevent duplicate records, invalid data and unauthorised access. 
The project is an academic prototype and will use fictitious information rather than 
actual motorists' personal information or live government records. The data model is 
designed to support the functions identified in Phase 2, including viewing fines, 
simulating payments, submitting disputes and generating reminders. Data protection 
is also considered in line with the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 
(POPIA), which establishes requirements for the lawful processing and protection of 
personal information (Africa, 2013). 
3.4.1 Data Sources and Data Classification 
The data required by NatiFine SA will originate from the following sources: 
• Motorist information: The motorist enters registration details such as an ID 
number, name, surname, telephone number, email address and password. 
Fictitious details will be used during development and demonstrations. 
• Fine information:  An authorised officer or administrator captures the fine 
identification number, motorist reference, issue date, due date, offence 
description, location, amount and photograph reference. 
• Payment information: The simulated payment module records the amount, 
payment date, payment method and transaction status. No actual banking 
transactions will take place. 
• Dispute information: The motorist provides the reason for disputing a fine and 
may attach supporting evidence. The administrator updates the dispute status. 
• Notification information:  The system generates simulated SMS and 
WhatsApp-style messages and stores their type, content and date in the 
notification history. 
• Security and access information:  The system stores account identifiers, 
password hashes and user roles to support authentication and access control. 
The data can be classified as personal, operational, financial, dispute -related and 
security information. Access must be restricted according to the user's role. For 
example, motorists should only access their own fine records, while authorised officers 
or administrators may access the records required to perform their assigned functions. 
The prototype will follow the principles of data minimisation and reasonable security 
safeguards. POPIA requires appropriate technical and organisational measures to 
protect personal information against loss, damage and unauthorised access or 
--- PAGE 8 ---
processing (Africa, 2013). 
3.4.2 Data Integrity Rules 
The following rules will be applied to maintain data accuracy and consistency. 
Entity integrity: Each database table must have a primary key that uniquely identifies 
every record. The primary keys are UserID, OfficerID, FineID, PaymentID, DisputeID 
and NotificationID. These keys must not be null or duplicated. 
Referential integrity: Foreign keys must refer to existing records. Fine.UserID must 
reference an existing motorist, and Fine.OfficerID must reference an existing officer. 
Payment.FineID, Dispute.FineID and Notification.FineID  must reference an existing 
fine. These rules prevent orphan records. Foreign -key enforcement must be enabled 
in SQLite. 
Domain integrity: Each field must contain data of the correct type and format. For 
example, fine amounts must be positive numbers, email addresses must follow an 
acceptable format, dates must be valid and status fields must contain approved 
values. 
Business-rule integrity: The system must prevent a motorist from paying the same 
fine more than once. In the prototype, each fine may have no more than one full 
payment record and no more than one dispute record. A fine with an open dispute 
must not receive an additional prototyp e penalty while the dispute is being reviewed. 
Once a payment is successful, the fine status must be updated to Paid. 
Security and confidentiality: Passwords must be stored as secure password hashes 
rather than plain text. Role -based access controls must prevent motorists from 
accessing another motorist's information or performing officer -only functions. These 
safeguards support the protection of personal information required by POPIA. 
Auditability: Important actions, including fine creation, payment attempts, dispute 
decisions and changes to licence -status flags, should be recorded with dates and 
times. Fine records should be marked as cancelled or otherwise updated where 
appropriate instead of being physically deleted, so that the prototype can preserve a 
history of relevant actions. 
3.4.3 Data Constraints 
The database will use the following constraints to reduce invalid or inconsistent 
records: 
• User.IDNumber: Required and unique, with exactly 13 digits for the South 
African ID -number format used by the prototype. Validation must check the 
format and, where implemented, the applicable checksum. Fictitious test values 
will be used. 
• User.Phone: Required and validated against an accepted South African 
mobile-number format. 
--- PAGE 9 ---
• User.Email: Required, unique and checked for an acceptable email format. 
• User.PasswordHash: Required and stored as a password hash using an 
appropriate password-hashing method. 
• User.LicenceStatus: Restricted to the prototype values Valid and Flagged. 
• Fine.Amount: Required and greater than zero. 
• Fine.PenaltyAmount: Must not be negative and will default to zero. 
• Fine.IssueDate and Fine.DueDate: Both required, with the due date later than 
the issue date and the issue date not in the future. 
• Fine.Status: Restricted to Unpaid, Paid, Disputed, Overdue or Cancelled. 
• Payment.FineID: A foreign key and unique value in the prototype, ensuring no 
more than one payment record per fine. 
• Dispute.FineID: A foreign key and unique value in the prototype, ensuring no 
more than one dispute record per fine. 
• Notification.FineID: A foreign key referencing an existing fine; multiple 
notifications may be associated with one fine. 
These constraints must be implemented in the database where possible, supported 
by application-level validation and verified through testing. The detailed field definitions 
are provided in the data dictionary in Section 3.8. 
3.4.4 Prototype Business Rules and Assumed Thresholds 
The reminder, penalty and licence -status rules are configurable assumptions 
introduced for the NatiFine SA academic prototype. They are not presented as official 
South African traffic -fine deadlines, statutory penalty amounts or legal licence -
disqualification rules. 
For demonstration purposes, the proposed system will apply the following rules: 
1. First reminder: A simulated reminder is generated when a fine remains unpaid 
for 14 days after its issue date. 
2. Additional penalty: A configurable prototype penalty is applied once when a 
fine remains unpaid for 30 days after its issue date. The penalty amount and 
calculation method must be defined in the system configuration. 
3. Licence-status flag: The motorist's prototype licence -status field is changed 
to Flagged when an eligible fine remains unpaid for 60 days after its issue date. 
This is only an internal demonstration flag and does not represent an actual 
change to a driver's legal licence status. 
4. Disputed fines: An additional prototype penalty is not applied while a dispute 
remains open. When the dispute is resolved, the fine's status and any 
subsequent processing must follow the configured prototype rules. 
--- PAGE 10 ---
5. Successful payment: Once a simulated payment succeeds, the fine is marked 
Paid, the payment record is retained, and no further unpaid -fine reminders or 
additional penalties are generated for that fine. 
6. Status restoration:  The prototype licence -status flag is cleared when the 
motorist has no remaining eligible overdue fines under the configured rules. 
The thresholds will be stored as configurable values so that they can be changed 
without redesigning the database. Actual traffic -fine procedures and applicable 
deadlines depend on the relevant legal framework and circumstances. The 
Department of Transport has also published implementation updates concerning the 
phased implementation of AARTO, demonstrating why the prototype's simplified rules 
should not be treated as a substitute for current official procedures  (Government, 
2025). 
These business rules provide a consistent basis for demonstrating reminders, 
simulated penalties and status updates during development and testing. All such 
actions will occur within the prototype and will not affect real fines, bank accounts or 
driving licences.

3.8.3 Normalization Check 
First Normal Form (1NF): every attribute holds a single atomic value and every table has a 
primary key; there are no repeating groups (multiple notifications and payments are separate 
rows in their own tables). 
Second Normal Form (2NF): all tables use a single-column primary key, so no non-key 
attribute depends on only part of a key. 
Third Normal Form (3NF): non-key attributes depend only on the key. Motorist details live 
only in User, officer details only in Officer, and Fine stores references (UserID, OfficerID) 
instead of copying names. The model is therefore in 3NF. 
Design note. PenaltyAmount is stored on Fine as required in Phase 2. It is derived from the 
Amount and the penalty rule, so it is deliberately recorded as a historical value (the penalty 
applied at that time) rather than recalculated, which protects the record if penalty rules change 
later.

Conclusion 
The Analysis Phase has shown how fines are handled today, why that process fails 
motorists and authorities, and what NatiFine SA must do to fix it. Twelve functional 
requirements, ten non -functional requirements, a set of integrity constraints and a 
normalized six-entity data model now define the prototype precisely. These outputs 
feed directly into the Design phase, where screens, database scripts and module 
structure will be produced, and into the Testing sprint, where each requirement will be 
checked.
